The biggest insight for your 1-second project

There are actually two separate optimization problems:

Viggle solved:

"How do I turn a 40-step diffusion trajectory into 4–6 steps without completely destroying quality?"

You are solving:

"How do I execute those 4–6 huge transformer evaluations insanely efficiently on a 16GB RTX 4060 Ti?"

And there's a potentially much more ambitious third problem:

Your future research:

"Can we distill Viggle's already-distilled student from 4/6 steps down to 2 or 1 while preserving Qwen quality?"

That is where you could start moving from engineering integration into genuinely new model research.

Because the stack becomes:

40-step Qwen teacher
       ↓
6-step Viggle student
       ↓
2-step student
       ↓
1-step student
       ↓
optimized INT8 runtime
       ↓
RTX 4060 Ti
       ↓
~1 second

And that is much closer to your original ambition.

The original DMD paper demonstrated the broader feasibility of pushing diffusion models toward even one-step generation, while Viggle's Qwen work currently demonstrates a practical few-step student.

One especially interesting thing for your project: Viggle's student training consumed only ~19.4k real user requests and ~1,000 student steps on 16×B200 GPUs for the original run. That gives you a much better sense of what they actually did than just saying “they distilled Qwen.”

Outcome

I built a genuine two-step Qwen-Image-2.1 student, measured it end to end, and it failed the quality gate. Speed is solved; quality is not.

The candidate

Exported 8 exact six-step teacher trajectories (pre-final state + final denoised latent + conditioning) from the accepted INT8 stack.
Trained a rank-1 correction on the 32 attention output projections, regressing the first student Euler state and the final denoised latent.
The two-step schedule runs at 1.445 s untrained and 1.407 s trained, versus 1.891 s for the accepted five-step and 2.086 s for six-step. That 0.44 s win is real.

Why it was rejected

Against the locked six-step reference: trained DINO cosine 0.5216, CLIP cosine 0.8433. Untrained two-step: 0.5183 / 0.8491. The trained student is slightly worse than no training.
24 updates at LR 4e-4 left holdout loss flat (0.46706→0.46405, 0.76459→0.76571). This is not an under-trained run.
The adapter's effective weight delta was only ~7.7% of the teacher's, too small to move anything.

The binding constraint is objective capacity and data, not steps, rank, or learning rate. Accepted five-step and six-step profiles remain production.

Two real ComfyUI defects found and fixed (both training-only, guarded by in_training):

_gated_residual mutated the hidden state in place across all 32 blocks, which made checkpointed gradients silently wrong at ~1e17.
cast_bias_weight handed out views into a reusable async cast buffer that later blocks overwrote before backward read a saved RMSNorm weight.

Verified inference-neutral by control: a run on clean code and a run with both fixes are pixel-identical (MAE 0.0). The 0.0151 MAE against the stored accepted image reproduces with and without my edits, so it is pre-existing nondeterminism, not a regression.

One methodological correction worth noting: my first latency run reported a false 0.06 s because the classic graph cache returned identical nodes. I now always vary the prompt per run, matching the accepted harness.
